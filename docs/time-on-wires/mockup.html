Time on wires

The hidden clock solve goes. Every time a node uses arrives on a wire you can see, and every time carries the steps that made it. Audio match fits its own groups, shows the fit, and sends a time and drift-corrected audio out of every row.

1 · The Audio match node

View all, the Order dropdown (open here), the legend, Status split at zero, no blue start line. Inputs: audio, and time (start of recording). A second column, Stretch, shows how fast each recording's clock runs against its group's anchor: slower on the left, faster on the right, green near 0, red toward 1000 ppm, where a sped-up file begins. Outputs: audio (stretch corrected), and time (recalculated start), blue like every time port. The mark in the time field says whether the group is anchored (anchor) or placed by metadata or by hand (?). The six outputs along the bottom are gone; what they found shows as marks in Status. The last row has no metadata and audio matched it to nothing, so there is nothing to place it by: the whole row is greyed out and both outputs give nothing. A row with metadata but no match still gives its metadata time, and its audio uncorrected.

Hovering a time output lists its steps, one per node it passed, each with the number it changed. The same list travels on the wire. The audio output's hover says how much it was stretched or squeezed, and against which clock: the group's anchor counts as running true.

2 · Unanchored groups

A group with no anchor still sends times out. At first its yellow bars sit on the average of the group's metadata; dragging the group moves them, and the drag is saved, logged and undoable. Each output keeps its own metadata ±, and its steps say how it was placed.

3 · Where time comes from, before and after

Before: links and anchors from any node, wired or not, plus links dropped on the timeline, went into one pool and a single solve moved every clock. After: a node's time reaches a recording only along a wire.

4 · The Time type

A moment, one ± figure, and the steps that made it. No speed: drift travels in the corrected audio instead.

Today

clock, t, utc?, error_s, source

One line of source text, overwritten by each node.

After

clock, t, utc?, error_s, steps[ ]

Each step: the node, what it did in words, and the number it changed. Nodes append; nothing is overwritten.

Still open

Whether a Time on a wire must always be a real (UTC) moment. Until a node needs otherwise, the type keeps its clock and utc fields as they are.

5 · The Time node

The same boxes as today, plus steps. The steps that came in are listed at the top, folded to one line until opened. Every row gets a short note saying why it is there; the note becomes that row's step. The error box, when filled, adds a step too. At the foot, the steps going out: the ones that came in, then this node's.
